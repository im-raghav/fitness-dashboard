Rules that keep you progressing (and uninjured)

    Body composition: muscle and abs

    Weight vs target

    Dots are daily weigh-ins (they swing about ±1 kg with water and food). The line is the 7-day average, which is what counts. The shaded band is where the current phase wants you to be.

    Fat mass

    Weight × body-fat %, 7-day average. This needs to come down for visible abs.

    Lean mass

    Everything that isn't fat (muscle, water, bone). Hold or grow this while fat drops.

    Body fat %

    From your smart scale. Home scales are rough in absolute terms, but the trend is meaningful. Back them up with a waist measurement.

    Weekly change

    Change in average weight from one week to the next. The shaded band is the current phase's target rate. Single weeks are noisy: a long run and carbs can add 1 kg of water.

    Waist

    The most reliable fat-loss check: measure at the navel, relaxed, once a week in the morning, and log it in the Health app (Browse → Body Measurements → Waist Circumference). It appears here automatically.

    Phases

    Strength

    Key lifts

    Main lifts: estimated 1-rep max

    Best set each session converted to an estimated one-rep max (weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)). Rising = getting stronger, which is the main driver of building muscle. Compound lifts only, because isolation weights in your log aren't comparable over time.

    Hard sets per muscle group per week

    Working sets from Lyfta (warm-ups excluded). The shaded band is each muscle's target for the current phase. Deadlift counts for both back and legs.

    Personal bests

    Best estimated 1-rep max, the set it came from, and your best in the last 30 days of logging.

    Weekly volume

    Total weight × reps lifted per week, in tonnes (weighted exercises only).

    From your Watch (every session, logged or not)

    Sessions per week

    Strength workouts recorded on the watch.

    Session length

    Minutes per strength workout. The shaded band (60–75 min) is the sweet spot: long enough for 12–14 hard sets, short enough to keep quality high.

    Estimated sets per session

    Counted from heart-rate peaks: each working set lifts HR, rest lets it fall. An estimate, not a log.

    Recovery between sets

    How far HR drops 60 s after a set peaks. Larger = better conditioning.

    Does lifting slow your running?

    Each run's efficiency (speed per heartbeat) compared with your usual, grouped by how long after lifting it was. Higher bar = a better run.

    Marathon

    Marathon

    Easy running

    Share of your running minutes in Zones 1–2. Target 80%: easy running builds endurance without leaving you tired for the next session.

    Long-run efficiency

    Speed per heartbeat on runs of 15 km+ (metres per minute ÷ HR). Rising = your engine is improving. Compare runs of similar length.

    Did each run stay under its heart-rate cap?

    Zone 2 runs: average under 148 bpm. Long runs: under 150 (except the race-pace rehearsal). "Time above" is the share of the run spent over the cap.

    Training load

    Fitness and fatigue

    Fitness is your 6-week training load, fatigue your last week. Fitness rising steadily = getting fitter. Load units come from minutes in each heart-rate zone (Z1 = 1 … Z5 = 5 points per minute).

    Form (fitness − fatigue)

    Below −30 for long = overreaching. −10 to −30 = productive training. Above +5 = fresh, good before a race.

    Load ramp (acute : chronic)

    Last week's load vs your 6-week average. 0.8–1.3 is the safe band; above 1.5 sharply raises injury risk.

    Running: aerobic engine

    Pace at fixed heart rate

    Median pace in minutes where your HR sat at this value. Lower = fitter. This is the cleanest progress signal there is.

    Weekly running distance

    Increase by roughly 10% a week at most; lifting already loads your legs.

    Intensity distribution (running minutes per week)

    Endurance improves fastest with about 80% easy (Z1–2) and 20% hard. Too much "moderate" (Z3) is the classic plateau trap.

    Aerobic decoupling (runs ≥ 30 min)

    How much efficiency fades from first to second half. Under 5% = your aerobic base handles that duration.

    Efficiency factor per run

    Metres per minute per heartbeat. Compare similar runs: higher = more speed for the same effort.

    VO₂ max (Apple estimate)

    Updates from outdoor runs and walks with GPS. Slow-moving; look at the trend over months.

    Running: form and speed

    Cadence

    Steps per minute. Higher (170–185) usually means less braking and impact.

    Vertical ratio

    Bounce ÷ stride length. Lower = more of your energy goes forward. Under 8% is very economical.

    Form fatigue

    Change in ground contact time, end vs start of run. Rising = form breaking down late.

    Personal bests

    Fastest segment of each distance from GPS tracks, and your best in the last 90 days.

    Speed: 1 km and 5 km (back in focus in the VO₂ max phase after the marathon)

    Best 5 km by month

    Fastest 5 km segment inside any GPS run that month (not only races).

    Recovery

    Illness early warning

    Three overnight signals that rise a day or two before you feel ill. Two at once = take it easy; resting HR plus one other = rest.

    Race-morning check

    Recovery after long runs

    Days until your resting HR and HRV are back to normal after each run of 18 km+. If this keeps growing, the long runs are too hard.

    HRV vs your normal range

    7-day average HRV against your 60-day normal band. Dropping below the band = accumulated stress; ease off or prioritise sleep.

    Resting heart rate

    Daily value vs 30-day baseline. +5 bpm for several days often means fatigue or illness coming.

    Overnight wrist temperature

    Change vs your 30-day normal. A jump of +0.5 °C alongside higher resting HR is an early illness sign.

    Breathing rate during sleep

    Very stable in healthy sleep; a rise of 1–2 per minute is a recovery or illness warning.

    1-minute heart-rate recovery

    How far HR falls in the first minute after a workout. Higher = fitter autonomic system.

    Sleep

    Sleep per night by stage

    Aim for 7–9 h asleep. Deep sleep drives physical recovery; REM supports learning and mood.

    Bedtime consistency

    Time you fell asleep each night. A steady time improves sleep quality as much as duration.

    Lowest heart rate during sleep

    A low, early dip means good recovery; late or high dips follow hard training, alcohol or late meals.

    EBC trek report

    Highest altitude each day

    From GPS on your recorded hikes.

    Blood oxygen (SpO₂)

    Daily median and lowest reading. 95–100% at sea level; 80–90% is normal above 4,500 m. A sudden drop with headache or breathlessness is the warning sign of altitude sickness.

    Resting heart rate

    Rises with altitude, then eases as you acclimatise. A higher-than-usual jump on a rest day means your body is struggling.

    Sleep

    Sleep is usually lighter and shorter at altitude.
